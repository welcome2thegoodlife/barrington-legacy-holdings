1. PURPOSE

Black Box™ is an animated, interactive, educational, musical, technological, and transmedia universe built around one central question:

«What if the knowledge we need for the future is hidden inside the past?»

The Black Box is both:

1. A fictional artifact inside the story.
2. A real-world framework for recovering knowledge, verifying it, transforming it into creation, and returning value to the future.

The fictional story teaches the methodology.

The real-world Creator Cube allows audiences to participate in it.

The larger system is:

RECOVER → VERIFY → ATTRIBUTE → CREATE → PROTECT → RETURN → TEACH → REPEAT

---

2. CORE LOGLINE

A group of young creators discovers a mysterious Black Box capable of opening pathways into the past.

Each journey allows them to encounter a forgotten innovator, educator, activist, scientist, artist, entrepreneur, inventor, or community builder whose knowledge was overlooked, erased, misunderstood, or left unfinished.

The children bring what they discover back to the present.

But they cannot simply copy the past.

They must understand it, verify it, credit it, and transform the knowledge into something new.

Using the Creator Cube, they turn recovered knowledge into music, technology, art, products, inventions, businesses, and educational experiences designed to help build the future.

---

3. THE CENTRAL IDEA

The Black Box does not merely recover the past.

It converts recovered knowledge into future capability.

The show therefore operates on two levels.

Story Level

Children go backward to recover knowledge.

Real-World Level

Viewers learn how to research, verify, create, protect, and contribute.

The audience is not merely watching the characters create.

The audience becomes part of the creation process.

---

4. THE BLACK BOX

The Black Box is an ancient-looking but technologically impossible object.

Its origin is intentionally mysterious.

It appears to contain no conventional power source.

Its exterior is simple.

Its interior appears much larger than its exterior dimensions should allow.

The Box responds to:

- Knowledge
- Questions
- Human intention
- Evidence
- Music
- Creativity
- Collaboration

The Black Box cannot simply be used like a normal time machine.

It requires a question.

The quality of the question determines the quality of the journey.

---

5. THE FIRST RULE

The Black Box cannot answer:

«"Show me something cool."»

It responds to meaningful questions.

Examples:

- Who invented this?
- What knowledge was lost?
- Who was not credited?
- What happened to this community?
- What did people know before this technology existed?
- What can we learn from this?
- What could we build differently today?

The children learn that curiosity is the key that activates the Box.

---

6. TIME-TRAVEL RULE

The Black Box does not allow unlimited manipulation of history.

The characters cannot simply change the past.

Their mission is recovery, not domination.

They observe, investigate, communicate, learn, and return.

This creates an important story rule:

The past is not theirs to rewrite.

They are responsible for understanding it.

8. THE LEGACY LOOP

Every major story follows the Legacy Loop.

STEP 1 — RECOVER

Find the forgotten knowledge.

STEP 2 — VERIFY

Determine what is supported by evidence.

STEP 3 — ATTRIBUTE

Identify the people, communities, creators, and sources connected to the knowledge.

STEP 4 — CREATE

Transform understanding into something useful.

STEP 5 — PROTECT

Credit and protect the modern creation and its historical lineage.

STEP 6 — RETURN

Use the resulting value to create additional opportunity.

STEP 7 — TEACH

Give the knowledge and methodology to the next generation.

STEP 8 — REPEAT

The next generation begins another loop.

---

9. EVIDENCE SYSTEM

The Black Box universe teaches children that not everything they hear is automatically true.

Historical information is organized into four levels.

Tier 1 — VERIFIED

Strong primary documentation and/or multiple reliable sources support the claim.

Tier 2 — STRONGLY SUPPORTED

Credible evidence exists, but additional verification may still be required.

Tier 3 — CONTESTED

Credible sources disagree or attribution remains unresolved.

Tier 4 — WORKING HYPOTHESIS / ORAL MEMORY

The information may represent cultural memory, oral history, or an emerging hypothesis that requires additional investigation.

The characters are allowed to preserve uncertain information.

They are not allowed to present uncertainty as established fact.

14. EPISODE ENGINE

A standard Black Box adventure can be structured as six acts.

ACT I — THE QUESTION

The children encounter a problem, mystery, missing invention, forgotten person, or unexplained piece of knowledge.

ACT II — THE SIGNAL

The Black Box activates.

The team discovers the historical destination.

ACT III — RECOVERY

The children enter the past and investigate.

They gather information, encounter the historical figure, and discover the deeper story.

ACT IV — RETURN

The team returns to the present.

They organize what they learned.

They separate evidence from assumptions.

ACT V — SYNTHESIS

The Creator Cube activates.

The children transform the recovered knowledge into a new creation.

ACT VI — LEGACY

The creation is shared.

The team asks:

How can this help someone else?

The episode ends by opening a new question for the next generation.

19. FICTION VS. HISTORICAL RECORD

Black Box maintains a strict separation between:

CANON

Official fictional events, characters, technologies, and story rules belonging to the Black Box universe.

HISTORICAL RECORD

Evidence-based information about real people, events, inventions, communities, and technologies.

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Claims that remain uncertain or contested.

Creative storytelling may dramatize historical events.

However, fictional dramatization must not automatically be presented as historical fact.

24. STORY ETHICS

Black Box must not exploit historical suffering simply for entertainment.

When difficult history appears, the story should seek:

- Accuracy
- Human dignity
- Context
- Attribution
- Age-appropriate presentation
- Multiple perspectives where appropriate
- Clear separation between fact and dramatization

The goal is not to make children afraid of history.

The goal is to give them the tools to understand it.
